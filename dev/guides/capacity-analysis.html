How capacity is analysed¶

What this guide covers¶

This guide states what the two capacity windows compute: the Capacity window, which works on a chain of trains, and the Capacity by area window, which works on the resources of an area. It gives their inputs, formulas and units. It also states what they do not compute, and what the area calculation of older versions did. It describes the program as it is, including its limits.

Place

Where to find it

The Capacity window

Diagrams > Capacity… and Run Results > Open result view > Capacity. Both need a completed run. A chooser titled “Capacity” asks for Route / corridor, Blocks, Analysis period, Cycle-closing train and the checked rows of “Trains on this route:”. Blocks offers each section of the route that the checked trains share, with the longest selected (Scope). It opens on the first route with a block on which two or more trains have complete occupations, with the trains of the first such block checked. The result window has the same title. Its first context line reads “Cycle X s, Y% of the P s period” (one decimal), and the next two name the section and the run. The toggle Calculation details below them shows “Cycle time X s / period P s × 100 = Y%” with the percentage to one decimal, as in the context line, and the trains that start and end the cycle; its tooltip gives their reference times. The tabs Pairs, Compression and Critical blocks follow. A tab title ends with its row count when the tab has rows. The context line “Section:” names the route, its corridor and the numbers of the first and last analysed block on the route, as in “Section: route0 (A), blocks 36 to 72”. Its tooltip gives their labels: “First block 36: B0 10.210 to 10.510 km” and “Last block 72: B0 21.010 to 21.235, B1 21.252 to 21.450 km”. The context line of the compressed chart starts with the same section. The tables name blocks by their ids, and a cell that holds ids has the labels and the headway figures as its tooltip. A cell that names a train has its reference time as its tooltip, such as “Reference time: planned departure at Guingamp”

The Capacity by area window

Diagrams > Capacity by area… and Run Results > Open result view > Capacity by area. Capacity by area describes it, its CSV area_capacity.csv and its options

The CSV

The button Export CSV… asks for a path and suggests the name capacity_analysis.csv. It writes a provenance file beside the CSV, named like the CSV with .provenance.json added. Its column Section names the blocks by their ids, as in “route0 / A / @35-B0@ to @71-B0@-21.235000/@0-B1@-21.252000”

The compressed diagram

The button Open Compressed Blocking Time opens the window Compressed blocking time. The window is built like the blocking-time diagram, with the same axes, block-section strip, legend, train filter, zoom and exports: Export CSV… (suggested name capacity_compressed_blocking_time.csv) and Export PNG…. It draws the capacity-critical envelopes amber and the recorded movement of each train moved as its occupations are (step 5), and no planned references. Its time axis spans the compressed envelopes with a margin at each end of 5 % of their span, at least 1 s. It starts below 0 s only when a compressed envelope does, and otherwise no earlier than 0 s

The input

The occupations: Diagrams > Blocking time… and Run Results > Open result view > Blocking time, and BlockingTimes.txt in the TrainTrajectories folder of the run output

The program computes no capacity of a line or a network. The percentage in the first context line of the Capacity window belongs to the chain of occurrences that the user selected. The Capacity by area window reports the occupation of the resources of an area by the trains that the user selected, and the figures are lower bounds when it says “partial traffic”. It states no trains per hour, and parity with the legacy tool is not established (Limits). The area calculation of older versions is not in the program (The area calculation the program no longer contains).

The Assignment workflow uses the Capacity window in steps 14 to 18. The signalling levels page has a section that separates signalling areas, block sections and the scope of this window.

Terms and units¶

All times are seconds on the time axis of the run. One simulation step is one second, as the delay guide shows.

Term

Meaning in the program

Occupation

The blocking time of one train on one block, from StartOccTime to EndOccTime. It starts at the start of the approach minus the setup time and the sight reaction time. It ends at the end of clearing plus the release time and the run-time margin. The blocking-time paragraph of Using EGTRAIN and authoring scenes says the same. Inputs gives the values

Resource

A block. Two occupations share a resource when a component of their block ids is equal. An id is split at “@” and its first and third non-empty parts are compared. An id without “@” is compared whole. An id has no direction of travel (shareBlockingTimeResource)

Scope

In the Capacity window, one route of the run and its analysed blocks: one section of the route, a run of consecutive blocks on which every checked occurrence has a valid occupation with a usable StartRunTime. A block where a checked occurrence has none ends a section (capacityStretchesFrom). Blocks lists each section in route order by the labels of its first and last block, for example “36: B0 10.210 to 10.510 km through 72: B0 21.010 to 21.235, B1 21.252 to 21.450 km (37 blocks)” for route0 of Paimpol, where the number before a label is the position of the block on the route. The tooltip of an entry names the blocks by their ids: “36:@35-B0@ to 72:@71-B0@-21.235000/@0-B1@-21.252000 (37 blocks)”. The list selects the one with the most blocks, the first of them on a tie. When a check box or the order of the list changes, the section that starts at the same block stays selected if there still is one, else the longest is selected. A new route selects its longest section. A section does not end where the order of the trains changes. The corridor name of a route is a label only. There is no corridor or network scope. The Capacity by area window has an area instead

Area

In the Capacity by area window, the blocks of a range of one route and, optionally, of a range of a second route. It is a list of resources of one analysis, not a signalling area, and it is not stored (Choosing the scope and the period)

Occurrence

A train of the run, named by its runtime id; the windows call it a train. It is listed when it has a valid occupation with a usable StartRunTime on a block of the chosen route, whatever route it runs on (step 1). An entry of the list reads “runtime id (operating code)”, or the runtime id alone when the train has no operating code or its code is the id

Order

The order of the checked rows of the list. The user can drag rows. The first order is the order in which the run holds its trains, not the order of time. The program never sorts

Period

A number of seconds entered in the chooser: default 3600, minimum 0.001, step 60. It is not read from the scene or the run, and it is not checked against the length of the run. There is no selection of a timetable window: every valid occupation of a selected train on the analysed blocks is used

Profile reference

The StartRunTime of the train on the first analysed block, in seconds. The result window calls it “Original reference”

Scheduled reference

A time that stands for the timetable, in seconds. Step 2 gives the three ways to get it

Inputs¶

  1. Occupations exist only for sections of level 0, 2, 3 and 4 (ComputeBlockingTimesInMixedSignallingAreas). A section of level 1 or 5, or without a level, gives none. A scene gives input only for the sections that a signalling area of level 0, 2, 3 or 4 contains. The committed scenes lists the areas of each committed scene. At levels 3 and 4 the run builds occupations per infrastructure element, and the line fixture of the characterization tests lists none at those levels; What is not established says so.

  2. An occupation is used only when it is complete, has a block id, has finite times and positions, starts at 0 or later, ends after it starts and has positions of 0 or more (validBlockingTimeDiagramInput). The window leaves other records out without a message.

  3. The setup time, the sight reaction time and the release time are not inputs of the window. The run passes fixed values and two settings of the case to the occupation routines (the call of ComputeBlockingTimesInMixedSignallingForAllTrains in app/DispatchController.cpp): a setup time of 5 s, a release time of 3 s plus the buffer time, a sight reaction time of 0.5 s, a safety margin of 50 that the routines of levels 0 and 2 do not take, an absolute running-time supplement of 0 and the recovery percentage. For a section of level 0 or 2, ComputeBlockingTimeForSingleLocation sets:

    • the start of the occupation to the start of the approach minus the setup time and the sight reaction time. For the first block of the route the approach starts at the start run time and both times are 0;

    • the end of the occupation to the end of clearing plus the release time. For the last block of the route the clearing ends when the head of the train leaves the block;

    • when the recovery percentage is not 0, a run-time margin that moves the end later: the run time of the block x the percentage / 100. With a percentage of 0 there is no margin and the routine writes a warning to the log.

    The case setting Buffer (s) (buffer_time_seconds, rounded to a whole number of seconds) is the buffer time of the release time. It is not the Buffer [s] column of the result. The case setting Recovery (%) (recovery_time_percent, rounded to a whole number) is the recovery percentage. The command-line options -b and -c replace the two settings (command-line options). The routines of levels 3 and 4 are separate functions, and this guide gives no formula for them.

  4. BlockingTimes.txt holds, for each train, its name and then five lines: the block ids, GeoPosStart, GeoPosEnd, StartOccTime and EndOccTime of its first N records. N is the number of blocking times that the run counted as complete (PrintTrainBlockingTimes). The window tests the complete flag of each record instead, so the two differ only when an incomplete record comes before a complete one. The file has no StartRunTime, so the analysis cannot be redone from the file.

  5. Not inputs: a stored analysis area and a timetable window. The Capacity window takes no description of the other traffic. The Capacity by area window reads the other trains of the run itself and reports those in the area as unselected traffic.

The calculation¶

The steps follow analyzeCapacity and the helpers of diagrams/CapacityScope.cpp. Times are in seconds.

  1. Candidates and blocks. A train has a usable record on a block when its first valid occupation that shares a resource with the block has a finite StartRunTime of 0 or more. The chooser lists every train of the run with a usable record on a block of the route. When it opens, it checks the trains of the first block of the route on which two or more trains have a usable record. The analysed blocks are the section selected under Blocks: a run of consecutive blocks on which every checked train has a usable record, ended by a block where one of them has none (Scope). capacityInputsFrom takes the record of each train on the first analysed block, which gives the profile reference of step 2. The occupations of a train are all its valid occupations that share a resource with an analysed block. The direction of the train is not examined.

  2. References. The profile reference is the StartRunTime of that record. The scheduled reference is, in this order:

    1. the authored time of the first stop, in stop order, that has one and whose route position is within 5 m of the start of that record: its departure, or its arrival when it has no departure (reference source “authored ScheduledDeparture at boundary station” or “authored ScheduledArrival at boundary station (departure absent)”);

    2. the scheduled entry time of the train plus (StartRunTime minus RunStartTime) (source “canonical scheduled entry + elapsed from RunStartTime”). RunStartTime is the second before the train first moves;

    3. the profile reference (source “profile reference fallback (scheduled entry unavailable)”).

    The CSV names the label and the source that applied (the columns “Reference label” and “Reference source”). The result window gives the reference time in the tooltip of a cell that names the train, as “planned departure at ” (rule 1), “planned entry time” (rule 2) or “simulated entry time” (rule 3).

  3. Minimum headway of an ordered pair. The leader is earlier in the order than the follower. Every pair of valid occupations, one of each train, that share a resource gives a candidate: (leader EndOccTime - leader profile reference) - (follower StartOccTime - follower profile reference). The minimum headway is the largest candidate, but not below 0. The resources whose candidate is within 1e-7 s of the largest are the governing resources. A pair with no shared resource has no minimum headway.

  4. Scheduled headway and buffer. Scheduled headway = follower scheduled reference - leader scheduled reference. Buffer = scheduled headway - minimum headway. It is not clamped: a negative value means that the scheduled references are closer together than the minimum headway. The Pairs tab lists the adjacent pairs of the order. The CSV lists every earlier/later pair.

  5. Compression. The first train keeps its scheduled reference as its compressed reference. Each later train gets the largest of two values: the compressed reference of the train just before it in the order, and, for each earlier train that shares a resource with it, that train’s compressed reference plus the minimum headway of the pair. The scheduled reference of a later train does not enter. It only gives the Shift column (compressed reference minus scheduled reference). The occupations of a train are moved by (compressed reference - profile reference). The compressed blocking-time diagram moves the recorded samples of the train by the same time and shows those that lie inside its moved envelopes. The earlier trains whose compressed reference plus minimum headway equals the compressed reference of the follower (within 1e-7 s) are its governing predecessors.

  6. Critical blocks. After compression, an occupation of a follower and an occupation of one of its governing predecessors that share a resource and touch are marked as critical (amber in the compressed blocking-time chart, as the legend under the plot says). They touch when the start of the follower minus the end of the predecessor is within 1e-7 s of 0. A touch is not an overlap. An overlap is an intersection of occupations of two trains on a shared resource longer than 1e-7 s. It is the conflict that the blocking-time chart draws in red (buildBlockingTimeDiagramSegments; the legend under the plot names the colour).

  7. Cycle. Cycle time = compressed reference of the cycle-closing occurrence - compressed reference of the first train. Cycle percentage = cycle time / period x 100. There is no upper bound. The user picks the closing occurrence in the chooser; it must be a checked row after the first. The program does not infer it.

  8. When a result exists. A result is shown only when it is analyzable: at least two trains, a valid profile reference for each (finite and 0 or more), a shared resource for every adjacent pair of the order, and no overlap among the compressed occupations of any two trains on a shared resource.

A worked example¶

The figures come from hand arithmetic on the blocktime lines of tests/characterization/expected/follow-level-0.txt and the planned departures of the first stop in tests/fixtures/scenes/line/services.json. Two trains, train 1 and train 2, run over the eight blocks of the line fixture at level 0. The scope is all eight blocks, the order is train 1 then train 2, the period is 3600 s and the closing occurrence is train 2. The characterization test runs this analysis on the same two trains and pins the minimum headway 471.5 s, the cycle 471.5 s, 13.097222 % and the critical block @3-B0@ in the capacity_pair line of follow-level-0.txt. That run takes the profile references as scheduled references, so the scheduled headway, the buffer and the shifts below are arithmetic by hand.

The first stop of each train is at the start of the first block (the station position and the first node of the track are both at 0 km), so the scheduled references follow rule 1 of step 2: the planned departures 120 s and 180 s. The profile references are the StartRunTime on @0-B0@: 59 s and 199 s. Each occupation in the lines ends 3 s after its clearing ends. Train 1 occupies the second block from 53.5 s, which is the start run time of the first block, 59 s, minus the setup time 5 s and the sight reaction time 0.5 s.

By hand, the minimum headways of the four pairs that share a block (A with B, A with C, B with C, C with D) are 20, 100, 5 and 10 s. The test asserts the compressed references 20, 100 and 110 s of B, C and D, and that A, not B, governs C. With C as the closing occurrence it asserts a cycle of 100 s, which is 50 % of a period of 200 s.

Special cases¶

Case

What the program does

No completed run

The Diagrams entry is disabled, and so is the button Open result view. If the function is reached anyway, the status bar shows “Run a simulation to open capacity analysis”

No block of any route with two trains that have a usable occupation on it

An information box: “No block has complete blocking times for two or more trains. Run a case with signalling data.”

An empty list or fewer than two checked rows

The list is empty when no train has a usable record on a block of the route or the route has no blocks. The chooser shows “Check at least two trains.” under the list, and OK is disabled until two rows are checked. A route with fewer than two listed trains opens with that message. The code after the chooser repeats the check with two boxes, “No block of the route is shared by the checked trains. Choose other trains.” and “Fewer than two trains share the chosen blocks. Choose other trains.”. The chooser accepts only two or more checked rows that share a block, so neither box follows an accepted chooser

Checked occurrences that share no block

The chooser shows “none” in Blocks and “No block of the route is shared by all checked trains.” under the list, and OK is disabled until the checked occurrences share a block

A change of train order along the route

The analysed blocks do not end there. The order of the list holds on every analysed block, and the program does not split them (Limits)

A closing occurrence that is not a checked row after the first

OK of the chooser shows “The cycle-closing train must be a checked row below the first.” and the chooser stays open. The first entry of the combo box, “Select the first train in the next period…”, is not an occurrence. The code after the chooser repeats the check with a box of the same text

Incomplete occupations

Left out without a message. A train without a usable record on any block of the route is not listed. A section ends before a block on which a checked train has no usable record, so every checked train has one on every analysed block. Nothing says why a section ends where it does

A repeated block

A train with two occupations that share a resource, other than the entry resource of the scope (next row): both are used, every pair of occupations is a candidate, the largest governs, and both move by the same shift

A scope that names an occurrence the analysis cannot tell apart

A box titled “Capacity” gives the reason and what to choose instead, such as “A selected block is also used outside the chosen blocks, so the passes cannot be told apart. Choose other blocks.”, and there is no result window and no CSV. The calculation matches occupations by resource id, so the window refuses a scope when a resource of the analysed blocks also occurs in a route section outside them, when the entry resource occurs in a second section among them, or when a selected train has several valid records for the entry resource. It never picks one occurrence. Repeated sections that the scope does not touch do not block it

Opposing trains

The Capacity window has no check of direction (the Capacity by area window has one). A train in the opposite direction whose records carry the same block ids is listed and, when checked, enters the pair and compression calculation like a following train. In tests/characterization/expected/sf-reverse-level-0.txt the trains run the line in the opposite direction and their records carry the ids @7-B0@ to @0-B0@. These are the ids that the trains of follow-level-0.txt carry, in reverse order

A period of zero, negative or not finite

The chooser accepts 0.001 s and more, so the window cannot start with such a period. analyzeCapacity reports a period that is negative or not finite as unavailable (-1) and keeps a period of 0. In both cases it gives no percentage

No shared resource for an adjacent pair, or an overlap after compression

The box “These trains do not form a chain: two neighbours share no block or still overlap after compression. Choose other trains or another order.” appears. There is no result window and no CSV

A percentage above 100

Shown as computed

The CSV is written only for an analyzable result. The window keeps the result it was opened with.

Capacity by area¶

The Capacity by area window reports, for an area that the user chooses and a period that the user enters, how much of the period the selected trains occupy each resource of the area. It also names the resource with the largest occupation and repeats the cycle of the chain of the selected trains. Its figures are occupation rates of the trains that were selected. They are not the capacity of the line. The window states no trains per hour, and parity with the legacy tool is not established (Limits).

Place

Where to find it

The window

Diagrams > Capacity by area… and Run Results > Open result view… > Capacity by area. Both need a completed run and are disabled without one. If the function is reached anyway, the status bar shows “Run a simulation to open capacity by area”. An options dialog titled “Capacity by area” opens first, and Cancel opens no window. The result window has the same title, is not modal and is deleted when it is closed

The context lines

The first line reads “Cycle X s, Y% of period” (one decimal), “Cycle X s, no valid period” without a valid period, “No cycle-closing train” without a cycle, or “Chain cannot be analysed” when the chain cannot be analysed. When the traffic is partial, the next line reads “Partial traffic: rates count only the checked trains” (Complete and partial traffic). The next lines name the area, the governing resource and the run. The area and the governing resource are named by the labels of their blocks. The tables and the CSV name resources by their ids, and a cell that holds ids has the labels as its tooltip

The tabs

Resources, Chain, Excluded traffic and Notes. The last two titles end with their row count when they have rows. A line above the tabs lists every status that occurs, with its count (“Statuses: Unselected traffic (1)”); without a status there is no such line. The headers Rate (% of period) and Union (s) have tooltips that give their rule

The CSV

The button Export CSV… asks for a path and suggests the name area_capacity.csv. It writes a provenance file beside the CSV, named like the CSV with .provenance.json added. The button is disabled when the area has no resource

The input

The occupations of the run, as for the Capacity window (Inputs)

Choosing the scope and the period¶

The options dialog has these fields.

Field

Meaning

Route / corridor, First block, Last block

One route of the run and a range of its blocks, in either order. Every block from the first to the last in route order is a resource of the area. The lists name each block by its position on the route and its label, “1: B0 0.000 to 0.230 km”, with the id as tooltip. Each resource is a block id; the corridor name is a label only

Second route, with Second route, first block and Second route, last block

A second block range. The area is the resources of both ranges. A resource that both ranges name counts once. “No second route” is the default

Analysis period

Seconds, default 3600, minimum 0.001, step 60. It is the denominator of every percentage. It is not read from the scene or the run, and occupations are not cut to it: the user chooses the trains that belong to the period

Trains in the area

Every train of the run that has an occupation on a resource of the area, usable or not, with a check box. All are checked at first. The order is the order of the reference time (the scheduled reference, else the profile reference; a train without either comes last, and equal times keep the order of the run). The user can drag rows. An entry reads “runtime id (operating code)”, or the runtime id alone when the train has no operating code or its code is the id, and ends with “, no reference time” for a train without one. Its tooltip gives the reference time, such as “Reference time: planned departure at Guingamp”

Cycle-closing train

“No cycle-closing train” (the default) or one of the listed trains. The program does not infer it

The scope can be one block, a stretch of one route, the whole route, or a stretch of each of two routes. A signalling area of the scene is not a scope, and no scope is stored.

The reference time of a train is taken at the first resource of the first range, by the rules of step 2 of The calculation. A train with no valid occupation there has no reference time, and its entry in the list says so. It still counts in the resource rows. The chain reports it with the status “Invalid reference”.

Under the list the dialog shows a message. OK is disabled, with the reason, when the run has no route (“The run has no route.”), or when the cycle-closing train is not a checked row after the first checked one (“The cycle-closing train must be a checked row below the first.”). A warning that does not disable OK says “The cycle-closing train runs in the other direction and is left out of the chain.” or “No train is checked.” (the window then reports no selected traffic).

What the window computes¶

All times are seconds. Percentages are percent of the period. For each resource of the area, the usable occupations of the selected trains that share the resource give:

Column

Rule

Trains

The selected trains with at least one such occupation

Occupations

The number of such occupations. A train that occupies the resource twice counts twice

Occupation (s)

The sum of EndOccTime minus StartOccTime over those occupations

Union (s)

The length of the union of the same intervals. Overlapping or touching intervals count once. When the selected trains occupy the resource at the same time, Occupation exceeds Union

Rate (% of period)

Occupation / period x 100. It can exceed 100

Status

“governing resource” for the resource with the largest Occupation, “no traffic” for a resource that no selected train uses, else empty

Two more results come with them.

  • Legacy envelope. The latest EndOccTime minus the earliest StartOccTime over all usable occupations of the selected trains in the area, idle time between trains included, and that value / period x 100. It applies the formula of the removed program (Formulas) to the selected trains of the area. The window does not show it; the CSV holds it in the columns “Legacy envelope[s]” and “Legacy envelope / period [%]”, for comparison with older work. It is not a capacity.

  • The chain. The selected trains that run in the direction of the first selected train, in the order of the list, go through the steps 2 to 8 of The calculation, with the usable occupations of the area as their occupations. The Chain tab holds three tables, Pairs, Compression and Critical resources, with the columns of the tabs of the Capacity window, a resource in place of a block. Its first line reads “Cycle X s of a P s period (Y%), from A to B”, and its tooltip gives the reference times of A and B. A cycle exists only when a cycle-closing train was chosen, it is a row of the chain after the first, and the chain can be analysed.

The direction of a train is the direction of the route it runs on (reversed_direction of the route in the run). The run records it, so the window does not infer it from the block ids.

The governing resource¶

The window uses the word in two places that must not be confused.

  • The governing resource of the area is the resource with the largest Occupation (s) of the selected trains. Resources within 1e-7 s of the largest are all governing, and a resource with an occupation of 0 is never governing. It is bold in the Resources tab, named in the context line Governing resource with its rate, and marked “yes” in the CSV column Governing. With no occupation that line reads “none”. It says which resource the selected traffic uses most. It does not say that the resource limits the capacity of the line.

  • The governing resource evidence of a pair is the resource that sets the minimum headway of two ordered trains in the chain (step 3 of The calculation). It belongs to the pair.

Complete and partial traffic¶

The CSV column Traffic says “complete traffic” or “partial traffic”, and the window adds the context line “Partial traffic: rates count only the checked trains” when the traffic is partial. The traffic is complete when all of this holds:

  1. At least one selected train has an occupation on a resource of the area.

  2. Every other train of the run that has an occupation on a resource of the area is checked. A train that is not checked gets the status “Unselected traffic”.

  3. No occupation on a resource of the area, of a selected or an unselected train, is unusable (incomplete, without finite times and positions, ending before it starts). Such an occupation gets the status “Incomplete occupation” and is used nowhere.

  4. No train appears twice in the lists. A repeat gets the status “Duplicate train” and is ignored.

Otherwise the traffic is partial, and the rates are lower bounds. Complete traffic means that the run holds no train or occupation in the area that the figures leave out. It does not say that the run holds all the traffic of the period. A train in the opposite direction that is checked counts in the resource rows and keeps the traffic complete, though the chain leaves it out.

Unavailable values¶

A value that the program cannot compute is not shown as 0.

  • A percentage without a valid period reads “-” in the Resources tab, and “no valid period” in the context lines and the Chain tab. It is an empty field in the CSV.

  • The cycle time and its percentage are empty without a cycle-closing train or when the chain cannot be analysed. The first context line then says so.

  • The legacy envelope is empty in the CSV without a usable occupation.

  • The headway columns of a pair that shares no resource are empty in the CSV.

  • The governing resource reads “none” when no selected train occupies the area.

  • A resource that no selected train uses has the rate 0 and the status “no traffic”. That is a computed value.

  • There is no trains per hour figure, in the window or in the CSV.

Reproducing a figure by hand¶

The two trains of the worked example run over the eight blocks of the line fixture. Take the whole line as the area, both trains as the selected traffic, a period of 3600 s and train 2 as the cycle-closing occurrence. The occupations are the blocktime lines of tests/characterization/expected/follow-level-0.txt, and the characterization test pins the results as capacity_resource lines.

For @3-B0@, the fourth block:

  • Train 1 occupies it from 190.5 s to 674 s, which is 674 - 190.5 = 483.5 s.

  • Train 2 occupies it from 342.5 s to 891 s, which is 891 - 342.5 = 548.5 s.

  • Occupation: 483.5 + 548.5 = 1032 s. Rate: 1032 / 3600 x 100 = 28.67 %. The window shows 28.7.

  • Union: the intervals overlap from 342.5 s to 674 s, so the union runs from 190.5 s to 891 s, which is 700.5 s.

  • On @4-B0@ the occupation is (748 - 246.5) + (965 - 666.5) = 800 s, and no other block reaches 1032 s, so @3-B0@ is the governing resource.

  • The legacy envelope is 1200 - 53.5 = 1146.5 s, because the earliest start is 53.5 s (train 1, @1-B0@) and the latest end is 1200 s (train 2, @7-B0@). The percentage is 1146.5 / 3600 x 100 = 31.8 %.

  • The chain is the one of the worked example: minimum headway 471.5 s, cycle 471.5 s, 13.1 % of the period.

  • No other train uses the area and no occupation is incomplete, so the traffic is complete.

On @0-B0@ the same rules give 87 + 87 = 174 s, 4.83 %, with a union of 174 s because the two trains do not overlap there. These are the first capacity_resource line of the file.

The area calculation the program no longer contains¶

Where to read it¶

The sources are in the Git history. Two commands show them:

git log --diff-filter=D --format=%H -- EGTRAIN/QEGTRAIN/simulation/Capacity.h
git show <that commit>~1:EGTRAIN/QEGTRAIN/simulation/Capacity.h

The callers were in simulation/Optimisation.cpp and app/DispatchController.cpp.

How it was meant to run¶

  1. The area file TrackLines/AreasCaseStudy.txt of the case input folder held one row per area: name, start, end, signalling level and an optional track line number. Start and end are in km. The track line number is -9999 when absent, which means all track lines. A row with a track line number names its area <name>_<number>.

  2. prepareSimulation called InitializeAllNetworkAreas for this file (in the last revision that had the code, only when the file existed). That set the level of the blocks and collected the block ids of each area.

  3. computeCapacityAndTphForAllNetworkAreas was to do the rest. For each area it selected the trains, computed the timetable variant and wrote TT_Statistics_Area_<name>.txt and TT_BlockTrainsInArea_<name>.txt. Then it compressed the trains and computed again. Last it added an area named EntireNetwork and wrote Statistics_Area_<name>.txt and BlockTrainsInArea_<name>.txt. The files went to AreaResults/<area>_Results under the folder that ComputeNetworkCapacityForTimetable received as its folder name.

In the history of the repository only the initialisation had a caller. computeCapacityAndTphForAllNetworkAreas was called only by ComputeNetworkCapacityForTimetable, as its last step: before it, that function reset the trains to the timetable and ran the train-level compression of the last row of Status of each piece on the trains of the run. No revision in the Git history of the repository called ComputeNetworkCapacityForTimetable (the history starts with an initial import). So no run of any revision in that history produced these files. The history also holds area files in the input folders of some older cases, and no output file of the calculation.

as above

The compression rule follows from computeEntryTimesToSolveConflicts. Call a the block of the later train and b the block of the earlier train. If a.start > b.end and a.end > b.end, the function returns the entry time of the later train - (a.start - b.end), which is that entry time + (b.end - a.start). Otherwise it returns that entry time + |b.end - a.start|. In that branch a.start <= b.end, because a.start > b.end together with a.end <= b.end would give a.end < a.start, and a blocking time that the run completed has EndOccTime not below StartOccTime. So |b.end - a.start| = b.end - a.start, and both branches give the entry time + (b.end - a.start). The shift of the later train is the largest of these differences. The removed code took the first M records of a train without testing whether each was complete (see the Membership row), so the single expression holds only for the records for which it was.

Two blocks matched when AreOnTheSameBlock returned true. It compared the same parts of the block ids as shareBlockingTimeResource does and had one more condition: when the first record of the pair carried a station name other than None, the second had to carry the same station name. As far as the code shows, only the routines of levels 3 and 4 copy a station name onto a blocking time, so the condition did not apply to records of levels 0 and 2.

A hand check of the envelope¶

Apply the envelope rule to the two trains of the worked example, with an area that spans the whole line, so that all eight blocks are in. The earliest StartOccTime is 53.5 s (train 1, @1-B0@) and the latest EndOccTime is 1154 s (train 2, @7-B0@). The envelope is 1154 - 53.5 = 1100.5 s. For N = 2 the trains per hour are 2 x 3600 / 1100.5 = 6.54. The ratio would be 1100.5 / times for whatever the simulation length was, and N / ratio / (times / 3600) gives the same 6.54 for every length.

This is the formula applied by hand to current records. It is not an output of the removed program. It answers a different question than the cycle of the worked example, which covers one chain after compression. The columns “Legacy envelope[s]” and “Legacy envelope / period [%]” of area_capacity.csv apply the same envelope formula to the selected trains and the resources of the chosen area, and divide by the chosen period. For these two trains and the whole line they hold 1146.5 s and 1146.5 / 3600 x 100, about 31.8 %. They give no trains per hour.

The label¶

The removed code carries the label “UIC code 406” for the compression, in comments in Optimisation.cpp, Optimisation.h and Simulation.cpp and in one console message in Optimisation.cpp. A comment in Capacity.cpp writes it “UIC Code 406”. The repository holds no description of that method. This guide therefore says neither that either calculation follows it nor that it does not.

Limits¶

  1. The percentage of the Capacity window covers the chain only. Trains that are not checked are not part of the figure, and the window does not say whether they use the same blocks. The Capacity by area window lists the trains of the run that use the area and are not checked.

  2. The compression depends on the order the user gives. The first order is the order of the run, not the order of time, and the program never sorts.

  3. No buffer or supplement is added after the compression. The Buffer column is the scheduled headway minus the minimum headway, and the scheduled reference of a later train does not enter the compression.

  4. The program compares the percentage with no threshold and makes no recommendation. It can exceed 100.

  5. Occupations exist only at levels 0, 2, 3 and 4 (Inputs).

  6. The setup, sight reaction and release times and the margin are fixed by the run, not by the window. The figures depend on them.

  7. The Capacity window does not examine the direction of a train (Special cases). The Capacity by area window leaves a train of the other direction out of the chain only.

  8. The Capacity window does not say why a section ends: the next block lacks a usable record of a checked train (Special cases). The Capacity by area window reports an unusable occupation but not a missing one.

  9. One analysis covers one section of one route. A block that a checked occurrence does not occupy ends a section, and the chooser offers each section that the checked occurrences share, with the longest selected first, so the parts of the route on either side of such a block are analysed one at a time. The program does not split a section where the order of the trains changes. The Capacity by area window takes a block range that the user chooses (Assignment workflow).

  10. The period is independent of the length of the run and of the timetable.

  11. Parity with the legacy tool is not established. No output of the removed calculation exists in the repository, and the two answer different questions (hand check). The legacy envelope of the Capacity by area window differs from the removed formula in what belongs to the area and in the denominator, and the program computes no trains per hour. Network-wide validity is not claimed: the program has no rule for which traffic a result must include.

  12. The program cites no norm or recommendation for the percentage or for a rate. Step 18 of the Assignment workflow refers to a course recommendation that comes from the course material and is not stated in this repository.

  13. The rates of the Capacity by area window count the trains that were selected, over a period that the user enters, with no time window. “Complete traffic” says that no train or occupation of the run in the area is left out (Complete and partial traffic). It does not say that the run holds all the traffic of the period.

  14. The governing resource of the area is the resource that the selected trains occupy most. It is not a bottleneck of the line.
