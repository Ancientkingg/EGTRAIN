Network renderer style map¶

The first imported renderer (472d0b5, mainwindow.cpp) is the railway-symbol reference. Preview, playback and replay share current scene geometry, inspection and layer controls. Canonical authored track/node geometry supersedes historical case-specific drawing branches.

Primitive

Historical function (472d0b5)

Presentation

Canvas

myQGraphicsView constructor: background rgb(0, 0, 0), no grid.

Background #101a22 with a faint #182832 grid in preview, live simulation and replay. Grid lines are cosmetic one-device-pixel lines on multiples of the spacing in scene coordinates, so they move with the scene while panning. Spacing starts at 80 scene units and is doubled or halved until it is 24 to 96 pixels on screen.

Speed-class track

arcDrawing with classifyTrackSpeed: cosmetic pen of width max(line_width, visual.width).

At least 200 km/h: RGB (30,130,210), cosmetic width 4; at least 120: RGB (160,160,160), width 3; otherwise RGB (120,120,120), width 2.

Connection

paintConnection: white, cosmetic, width line_width. The drawing code adds every connection before the track lines.

White cosmetic width 2, drawn below the track lines.

Segment/switch node

paintNode: light-gray ellipse of size node_size, cosmetic hairline pen; size 0 at the arc starts of a track with four nodes. In Input/Input_EGTRAIN_Banedanmark a track with four nodes draws only its middle arc and no last node.

Shared preview/runtime scene-sized light-gray circular dot, without a device-pixel minimum. The current runnable-chain rule uses an ordinary first point and zero painted size for ordinary starts on four-node tracks; the final endpoint remains visible. A track whose view sets double_switch paints no ordinary dot and draws neither its first nor its last arc. Transparent device-space hit geometry retains semantic picking without painting a minimum dot.

Station/platform node

paintStationNode with classifyStation; paintStationPlatform for the white platform bars in passenger-GUI mode.

Shared preview/runtime square at later station endpoints. A square with three or more connections is the interchange blue (80,120,210); the square at the end of a line with a platform is the platform dark gray (70,70,70); every other square, including an interior station square with a platform, is the ordinary gray (120,120,120). White platform bars follow passenger-GUI mode; preview has no passenger counters. Station and name layers do not hide structural squares.

Station name and pictogram

paintStationName: white text, font size / 5, a space before every capital after the first letter. paintStationIcon: raster station_pixmap scaled to station_size.

White name, font station_size / 5 at scene scale, centered at the authored station anchor plus its derived decoration offset. Above it a light (232,238,242) mark with a dark (16,26,34) halo of 2 logical pixels, painted as vectors at the device resolution: a bar for a major station (three or more connections at its node), a disc for a station (at least one platform) and a ring for a halt (no platform). A run without the shared preview does not count connections, so it draws no bar. It is parented to its authored structural node where available for artwork hits. Both keep their anchor point (bottom centre of the pictogram, top centre of the name), offset and parent at every zoom. Their size on screen is the larger of the scene size and a minimum: 24 px high for the pictogram and a 12 px font for the name. Names that overlap on screen are hidden in order of selected, followed, platform count, then the existing tie breaks; the pictogram and its hit target stay. Preview artwork keeps its own canonical station identity; position-only artwork has no invented node. The map key has one row for each class on the canvas (“Major station”, “Station”, “Halt”); the map key and menus use the same marks in a dark variant.

Signal

paintSignal: white post and base, head of size node_size with a hairline pen (width 0), one head per direction. A head starts green; updateSignalAspect sets green for codes 270 and 180, yellow for 75 and red for 0, and keeps the colour for any other code.

Each authored direction has its own scene-sized aspect head, white cosmetic post and white base. No viewport slots, grouping, sectors, multiplicity labels or direction ticks. Transparent device-space hit geometry retains signal inspection at Fit: a click on it selects the signal even where a track or connection lies under it, while station artwork keeps its priority. Selection does not overwrite aspects. A head has one of five states, with one text for the tooltip, the inspector and the end-to-end checks: Stop (red, codes 0 and 751), Caution (yellow, code 75), Proceed (green, codes 180 and 270), Unavailable (an empty gray ring, RGB (150,150,150)) and Failed (a red lamp with a white cross). A head is unavailable when its section has no signalling level, when no route runs through its section in its direction, and for a code that no aspect uses. A head is failed, on every head of the failed section that has a state, for the steps in which a signal failure incident is active. Levels 3 and 4 keep red and green. Heads in the preview before a run stay green. A lit head (every state except Unavailable, whose gray ring has its own stroke) has a black outline of one device pixel at every zoom. Marks add a second cue to the colour: a dark bar on Stop, a dark dot on Caution, a short gray dash in the Unavailable ring, the white cross on Failed; Proceed has none. A head draws its mark only when it is at least 6 device pixels wide; narrower heads show colour only. The map key has a row for each of the five states.

Train and passengers

paintTrain: one polygon per wagon, outline pen width 3, colours from classifyTrainType (keyword colours for freight, high speed, sprinter and intercity, a default yellow otherwise). Passengers: pax_icon.png scaled to station_size / 10. updateTrainPosition: a black addSimpleText speed label for each train, such as “102 km/h”, at the centre of the train plus (6,-18) scene units.

Historical train polygons in one default yellow, (235,210,55) with outline (110,90,20), whatever the rolling-stock type; the keyword colours of classifyTrainType are not used. A body with its outline is at least 8 logical pixels thick on screen: where the view shows it thinner, it is stroked in its fill colour to that thickness with round corners, and the stroke is part of its click target. Above that zoom the body is drawn as authored. A train whose service has a valid visualization_color (Services dock, Service visualization colour) is drawn with that fill and its darker shade as the outline, for every occurrence on the canvas and in replay; an unknown service or an invalid text gives the default. The map key has a “Train” row in the default yellow when a train uses it, then one row per other fill colour labelled with the service ids that use it (the first three, then “and N more”; the tooltip lists all), ordered by the smallest service id of each row. Original scene-sized pax_icon.png passenger glyph, also used by the map key. Passenger information callouts remain separate operational overlays; no train category badge is drawn. From a zoom of 3x, and for a selected or followed train at any zoom, the speed of the train, such as “102 km/h”, is drawn beside it in white 8 pt text with a dark (16,26,34) outline of 1 logical pixel. The text starts 8 logical pixels right of the centre of the train, the bottom of its line is 8 above it, it keeps its size at every zoom and it takes no click. Train speed labels hides it.

Selection

myQGraphicsColorizeEffect(Qt::blue, 1) on the clicked item.

A ring in the cue colour (61,214,255) with a dark casing, drawn in screen pixels just below the item in the stacking order, so it keeps its size at every zoom. A line item gets a halo along its line, any other item a rounded ring of at least 16 by 16 pixels with 3 pixels of padding; a station node is ringed together with its pictogram. The ring never recolours or covers the item, is covered by the item and by everything stacked over it, and takes no mouse input.

Track state

updateBlockOccupationStatus: a red myQGraphicsColorizeEffect on the blocks occupied by a train. releaseBlockOccupationStatus: a white effect on every track at the start of each step of updateTrainPosition. Prepared and blocked do not exist.

In a run and a replay TrackLineItem::paint draws a track line that a train occupies, every arc of the block section the train is in, in red (255,0,0) instead of its speed colour, at the width of its speed class. The red ends with the run, after Stop and when the run fails; a replay shows the occupied lines of its frame. Prepared and blocked are not painted. An occupied line in a section that a signal failure blocks is red as well, and the arc inspector shows it as blocked; otherwise the arc inspector shows occupied, blocked or prepared. The map key of the run canvas has an “Occupied section” row, also after the run ends, and the key of the preview has none.

Intentional differences from the historical renderer¶

These are the only intentional differences. A difference from the reference that is not listed here is not intended. The numbers are the pull requests that made the change, or the issue where none did; Historical track presentation gives the detail and the captures.

  • Station pictogram and its size on screen (#579, #822): a mark per station class with a dark halo replaces the raster pictogram, and the pictogram and the name have a minimum size on screen.

  • Canvas (#509): a soft background and a faint grid replace solid black.

  • Selection (#606): a ring replaces the blue recolouring of the selected item.

  • Train colours (#549, #555, #572): one default colour replaces the keyword colours, and a service can set its own colour on the canvas and in the result windows.

  • Signal head states (#586): an empty ring for a head without signalling data, a red lamp with a white cross for a failed head, and a dark bar or dot on Stop and Caution. A lit head keeps the hairline outline of the reference.

  • Presentation scale (#422): symbol sizes, text and offsets are converted from the historical construction units.

  • Mainline track grey (#822): track of 120 km/h and above is (160,160,160) instead of (80,80,80), which is below the 3:1 contrast target.

  • Train size on screen (#822): a train body is at least 8 logical pixels on screen, so a running train stays visible at Fit. Signal heads keep their 6 pixel rule.

  • Station names (issue #387): a space goes before a capital only after a lowercase letter or at the end of an acronym.

  • Train speed: the speed beside a train is white with a dark outline, keeps its size on screen and is shown from a zoom of 3x or for a selected or followed train. The reference drew a black label at scene scale beside every running train.

  • Track states (issue #387): blocked and prepared sections are shown in the inspector and not painted. An occupied section is plain red (255,0,0) at the width of its speed class, where the reference tinted it with a red colour effect.

  • Running state (issue #387): the white repaint of every track at each step is not reproduced.

Operational prepared and blocked states remain in scene data and inspectors, but do not change ordinary historical track paint; an occupied line is red in a run and a replay. Selection draws a ring around the item and never recolours it, so a selected track keeps its speed-class color. The exception is the preview, where the track of the row selected in the Infrastructure table is drawn in the cue colour, width 4. A refresh of the table keeps a selected row and selects none otherwise, so Run, Stop, Save and New case draw no track in it unless the user selected a row. The cue colour is reserved: no built-in track, signal or default train colour uses it; a service colour chosen by the user is not checked. The map key shows only visible track styles. Layer/Follow/replay controls are unchanged.

Preview uses canonical read-only fields and marks runtime-only information unavailable before Run. Signal head identity is the rendered canonical track, section and direction, plus any authored signal IDs, not a numeric runtime placeholder. Runtime numeric inspection remains separate. Expanded hit shapes do not determine preview topology bounds or Fit. These implementation and semantic checks do not establish matched-scale historical visual acceptance.

Scene-space presentation distances use the fixed measured historical construction convention Q_h = 800000 * 100 / 360, converted by TrackPreview’s actual normalization S_c / Q_h. This includes artwork/text geometry and annotation nudges, node/signal dimensions, platform/passenger adornments, train lateral thickness and callouts. It does not rescale physical interpolation or cosmetic strokes. Fonts retain their original metrics through fractional item scaling. Only wholly successful visible authored station-view projections enable conversion; raw, mixed, invalid, empty, degenerate or nonfinite scenes keep factor
