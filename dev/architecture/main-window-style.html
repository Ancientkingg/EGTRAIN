Main window¶

Playback¶

The Pause button reads Resume while a run is paused. It is as wide as the longer text, so the toolbar does not move, and its outline shows the whole button, so the gaps from Run to Pause and from Pause to Stop are equal.

The replay row starts with the icon buttons Start, Play and End, each with a tooltip and an accessible name; Tab goes from Start to Play and End and then to the slider. While the replay plays, Play shows the pause icon, the tooltip and the name Pause. The icons have one size, so End and the slider do not move. The label after the slider shows the clock time of the shown frame in the format of the progress bar and is as wide as its longest text, so the slider keeps its width while the user seeks.

The speed slider in the toolbar sets how fast a run plays; right is faster. The filled part of the track, #315a70, shows how far the slider is towards the fastest setting; the rest of the track is #7b8b96. The handle is a solid #315a70 circle with a white border. It turns #26495c under the pointer and while it is dragged, gets a #24313a border when it has keyboard focus, and the handle and the filled part turn #9aa5ab when the slider is disabled. The replay slider in the replay row has the same look.

The readout next to the slider shows the current speed as Fastest or as a factor of real time such as 4.0x or 100x. Its width fits the longest text, so the toolbar does not move while the slider is dragged. The slider has no Slower and Faster words beside it: with them the toolbar needs about 1070 px and no longer shows every control in a 1024 px window.

Toolbar buttons and Follow¶

Every toolbar button has the face of a push button: a white fill, a #7b8b96 outline and a 6 px radius. Under the pointer the outline turns #5b6871, while pressed the fill turns #e3eaee, and with keyboard focus the button has a 2 px #2e7491 outline with 1 px less padding, so its text and icon do not move. A disabled button, such as Pause and Stop before a run, has a grey #eceeec fill, a light #c9d3db outline and grey text. Open Case keeps its darker #e3eaee fill and bold text.

The two group separators of the toolbar are 1 px wide lines as tall as the toolbar row less the margins of the style sheet, at every window width. A menu separator is a 1 px #c9d3db line, the colour of the menu border, at every scale.

A tooltip names the shortcut of its button as the platform writes it, such as Ctrl+R, or ⌘R on macOS.

Follow shows a crosshair icon beside its text. While Follow is on, the button has a light #d3e3ec fill, a #315a70 outline and dark text; under the pointer the fill turns #bcd2df and the outline #24313a, and with keyboard focus it has the 2 px #2e7491 outline of the other toolbar buttons.

The closed train list ends a name that does not fit its 180 px with an ellipsis; the open list and the tooltip show the whole text.

The status bar shows the zoom of the network view as Zoom: Fit for the whole network and as a factor such as Zoom: 2x otherwise. The Follow label beside it shows one clause, such as Rail-1 enters at 08:25:50 or Following Rail-1, at most 400 px wide and elided with an ellipsis beyond that; its tooltip gives the whole explanation. The left of the status bar does not repeat that clause: it names a change, such as Follow off when Follow switches off, and a run that ends says Simulation complete. A message wider than the room left of the fields on the right ends in an ellipsis.

Run results header¶

The dialog Run simulation shows the duration of the run as H:MM:SS, the format of the Travel time column of Run Results, followed by the clock time at which the run ends, as in 2:30:00, until 09:40:40.

The text at the top of Run Results puts each entry on a line of its own: Run: <case> / <scenario>, Occurrences: ... and Status: Completed. With advanced details on, the direct incident evidence and Destination terminations: <count> follow, and Delay baseline: <run> comes last when a baseline is set. A long entry wraps inside its own line.

When Run Results first opens at the bottom of the window it takes a third of the window height, or the height that its content needs with the table header and three rows when that is more, but at most about half of the height that it shares with the network view, so the network and the replay row keep the rest. A dock lower than its content scrolls, and the table shows at least its header and one row. The delay comparison follows the table, so that in a maximised window on a 1024 by 768 screen the table header and a row show without scrolling. A height that the user drags is kept while the dock stays open; a new run that opens the closed dock sets that height again.

The Case and Layers dock opens at the width of its widest layer toggle, Stations and platforms, plus the vertical scroll bar. A case name up to that width fits on one line, and a longer one wraps. The user can drag the dock wider.

The toggle that opens the map key has the white fill, #7b8b96 border, 6 px radius and states of a push button, with small grey bold text, and keeps that face while the key is open. The map key has a Passenger count row only while the network shows passengers: the run started with passengers on, the case has passengers and the Passengers layer is on.

The editor docks share one group of tabs on the right of the window. The group takes the whole height between the toolbar and the status bar, and Run Results and the other docks below the network end at its left edge, so each open editor shows several rows of fields, also after a run in a short window. Choosing an editor in the Editors menu opens it, or brings it to the front when it is open behind another tab. An editor dock, such as Rolling Stock Units, opens at least as wide as its size hint, also after another dock has been dragged narrower, so its fields do not scroll sideways. An editor dock floated with its button or a double click opens inside the screen, as large as its content asks where the screen allows. In a narrow group the names of the tabs end in an ellipsis, each tab keeps at least 3 em for its name, and the tooltip of each tab gives its whole name; when the tabs still do not fit, the bar scrolls. Two scroll buttons with the face and border of a tab then end the bar; each shows a #5b6871 arrow of the size of a drop-down arrow, turned to the side, which is #9aa5ab when the bar cannot scroll further that way.

The Services dock shows its list above its tabs, so that beside Case and Layers in a window of 819 px it needs no horizontal scroll bar. Tabs that do not fit scroll and keep their whole names.

The lists of Rolling Stock Units, Services, Incidents and Entrance delays show about three rows, with their three buttons in one row below, so that the fields of the selected item keep the height of a short dock. The title of the dock names the list of Rolling Stock Units and the list of Services. The list of Services keeps its height on the Timetable tab.

Light look¶

EGTRAIN has one light look, also when the system uses a dark appearance. On macOS the application keeps the light Aqua appearance, so native controls, menus, dialogs and title bars stay light. On other systems a dark system palette is replaced by the light standard palette of the widget style, or by the light palette of Fusion when the style follows the dark desktop as Fusion does from Qt 6.5, at startup and whenever the desktop hands over a dark palette later, as a dark XDG desktop portal does, so selected rows, tooltips and charts stay light; Qt 6.8 and later is also asked for the light colour scheme. The title bar belongs to the window manager outside macOS and can still follow a dark system setting.

Widget style on Windows¶

Qt 6.7 and later give Windows 11 the windows11 widget style. EGTRAIN takes windowsvista, the style of Qt 5 that its windows were laid out in: with windows11 the menu bar is 33 px tall instead of 22 px at 100 %, spin boxes are 2 px taller, and with Paimpol open on a 1024 x 768 screen Case and Layers needs a scroll bar. The -style option and QT_STYLE_OVERRIDE still choose another style.

Focus and buttons¶

A focused push button shows a 2 px #2e7491 border and no rectangle around its text. The filled default button of a dialog shows focus as a 2 px #0b2430 border. In Run simulation only Run simulation is filled: Cancel keeps the plain face when it has the focus, although Enter then presses it.

A button that opens a menu, such as Open result view… in Run Results and Trains in a result window, keeps 20 px on its right for the arrow. Trains, Occurrences and Stations in the result windows have the white face, border, radius, states, height and font size of the push buttons beside them. macOS gives a tool button a smaller font, so TrainFilterButton takes the application font. A drop-down list, such as the train list beside Follow, draws its arrow without a frame of its own. The train list beside Follow opens as wide as its longest entry; where the style draws the list as a menu, as on macOS, that width includes the room it keeps for a check mark before each entry.

Tables and progress bar¶

Tables follow Interface style. Their colours are set in the stylesheet, so a dark system theme does not darken them under the dark text.

In the Run Results table the train and the operating code are left-aligned; the numbers and times are right-aligned.

The timetable table colours a delay above 60 s #81352d, a delay above 0 s #74451c and any other delay #006400. Each has a contrast of at least 6.9:1 on both row colours.

The progress bar of a run fills in #315a70. Its text is #24313a on the empty part, 11.4:1 on #eceeec, and white on the fill, 7.4:1: TimeProgressBar draws the text itself, 6 px inside the edges of the bar, and in the stylesheet’s selection-color over the fill. A new step of the run shows at the next paint of the window: the bar does not paint at once, as that paint would also draw the network view in the middle of a snapshot delivery.

In Run Results the Open result view… button has the white face, height, text size, grey border and padding of the Export CSV… and Export PNG… buttons beside it, the same #5b6871 border under the pointer, 2 px #2e7491 border with focus and #e3eaee fill while pressed, and the same grey face when disabled.

Keyboard¶

Every menu of the menu bar has an Alt mnemonic: File, Edit, View, Editors, Simulation, Help and Diagrams. macOS does not show them.

In Run Results, Tab reaches Open result view…, Export CSV… and Export PNG… before the table, and leaves the table for the delay baseline buttons after it; the arrow keys move between the cells. The tables of the editors work the same way, and while a cell is being edited, Tab moves on to the next cell. The Run Results table has a #c9d3db border that turns #315a70 while the table has the focus. The lists and tables of the editors, the Scene Validation table and the Loaded Data tree have a #c9d3db border that becomes a 2 px #2e7491 border with the focus, and their rows stay in place. A tab bar inside an editor, such as the tabs of Rolling Stock Units, shows the focus on its selected tab: a #2e7491 face inside a 2 px #0b2430 border, with the name in place. The tab bars of tabbed docks, the scroll buttons of all tab bars in the docks and the scroll areas of the docks and their panels are not in the Tab order: Tab reaches the controls inside a dock, and the menus raise a dock. Tab and Shift+Tab pass over the docks behind the front tab of a group, which Qt keeps in the window out of sight; a floating dock has a Tab order of its own. The network view takes the arrow keys and the zoom shortcuts, and its 2 px #3a4a54 border turns #8fbcd4, 4.5:1 with #3a4a54, while it has the focus.

Some platform behaviour is left as it is. Space activates a focused toolbar button and Enter does not, the platform convention for buttons. The native file dialog on Windows follows the dark mode of Windows while the window stays light. On Windows, QT_SCALE_FACTOR enlarges the text but not the check box indicators of the default windowsvista style, while Windows display scaling enlarges the indicators and the icons as well as the text (#955, #956).
