Detailed Description

Draws the track preview and the items of a run on the network canvas and keeps track of the items on it. The scene owns every item; the lists, maps and pointers below only observe them and are never used to delete one. They are public until all the code that fills them is in this class.

clear() deletes every item of the scene and then forgets all the pointers to them. A pointer that stays after the scene is cleared would dangle, so the registries belong with the code that clears the scene.

Positions and sizes are in scene units. The preview puts a track line at the normalized coordinates of the track preview. A run without the preview puts it between its stations, which are projected from their latitude and longitude or else placed at their position in kilometres times 1000. A track line in a region of fewer than two stations is at its position in kilometres times 1000. A scene unit therefore has no fixed length in metres. A train is given by its index in the run.

◆ stepTrains()

void NetworkPresenter::stepTrains ( )

Moves every live train that is in motion to where it is at the moment, and tells the host where a promoted one is drawn. A train far outside the visible scene is drawn again only at the next update or when it stops. A timer of the presenter calls it about once a frame while a train moves, at a pace that the updates do not change. A live update does not move a train on its way that the last step drew; a test calls it with the clock of setClock().

◆ updateTrainPosition()

void NetworkPresenter::updateTrainPosition ( const GuiSimulationSnapshot & snapshot,
int t,
Playback playback )

Adds, moves and hides the trains of the snapshot at step t, in seconds from the start of the run, and sets the state of the sections and of the occupied arcs on the track lines. apply() does this; it is public so that the trains can be updated alone.

A live train glides along its route at an even speed from where it is drawn to its new place, drawn wagon by wagon where it is at the moment, as getTrainPolygon() draws it at an update, with its badge and its callouts. The glide lasts several recent update intervals, up to a bound, so that the next update, which starts the next glide from where the train is then drawn, gives it a speed close to the one before, also when the updates come unevenly or bring uneven steps of simulated time; the speed still changes a little at each update. An update does not move a train on its way that the last step drew: the next glide starts where that step drew it, at the time of that step, so that on screen only the steps of stepTrains() move it, each as far as the time since the step before. A train that the last step did not draw, as when the event loop is busy or the train is far outside the visible scene, goes on from where its glide is at the update. The train trails its newest place by a few updates. At the highest speeds that is a long way, so the occupied track and the signal aspects, which show the newest state, can run well ahead of the drawn train. An update that leaves the train at its place keeps the glide that runs, and without a new update, as at a pause, the train also goes on to that place; it stops there when the glide ends. A train that has just appeared, has another route or direction, runs backwards, or has moved further than the visible view since its last update is placed at once, and so is every train of a replay.
