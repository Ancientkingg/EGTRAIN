Detailed Description

Where the ticks and labels of a chart go. The rules work on numbers, so they need no chart and no fonts: the caller reads the axis, measures the text and applies the result.

The ticks of a clock axis from low to high, as (elapsed seconds, label) in order, interval apart (clockTickInterval) with startOffset the clock time of elapsed zero in seconds since midnight. Ticks fall on round clock times. Elapsed zero, the run start, keeps a tick of its own while it is in range, and a round tick less than a step from it is left out where labelRoom of its label is more than its distance at pixelsPerSecond, so where the two labels would overlap, or while the plot has no size. A tick on a whole minute reads HH:MM while the ticks are a minute apart or more; every other tick reads HH:MM:SS.

◆ labelledStations()

std::vector< std::pair< double, QString > > labelledStations ( const std::vector< StationTick > & stations,

double high,
bool reverse,
const QRectF & plot,

double chartWidth,
const ChartTextWidth & textWidth,
const std::function< QString(const QString &text, double width)> & elide )

The label each station gets above the plot, as (position, plain text), on an axis from low to high that runs right to left with reverse. Every label is centred on its station, must lie within the chart and may not touch its neighbour. The chart hides a label whose text starts left of labelsLeft, the left edge of its axes. The chart shortens a label that is wider than the share of the plot width that it gives each of the labels, so a label is only used when it is narrower than that share. Of the stations in the range, every first, second, third and so on is tried until the labels fit. That gives the number of labels. When stations have calls, that many labels go to the stations with the most calls, as long as the labels keep apart; between choices with as many calls the stations of the stride win. A station gets the longest spelling that fits. When no label fits whole, the middle station keeps its shortest spelling, shortened by elide to half the plot width less a margin.

The ids that the strip shows across plot, in rows rows of rowHeight from top. marks has one entry per section, xAt gives the pixel of a distance along the route, and idWidth the width of the id of a section, in bold when strong. A plot of 8 px or less shows none. Each id is centred in the part of its cell that lies in the plot and kept inside the plot, and goes in the first row where it keeps clear of the ids placed there. The ids are placed in route order when that names every section. Otherwise the ids of critical and conflict sections are placed first, then those of the first and last section, of switch sections and of station sections, then the others in route order, and an id that keeps clear in no row is left out. The result has the order of placing. candidates gets the number of ids that could be shown.

◆ widenedTickRange()

std::optional< std::pair< double, double > > widenedTickRange ( double low,
double high )

The range low to high widened to round ticks past its ends, so that both ends carry a label and a line at an end keeps clear of the plot edge. Each end moves out to the first tick beyond it; an end within a thousandth of the range of a tick counts as on it. A range that starts at zero keeps zero as its start, also when it starts a little below zero, as a chart builder's margin of 0.01 km does: up to a thousandth of the range or a tenth of the step. The step is the smallest one that keeps the widened range to eight steps, which is the step that roundTickPlan gives the widened range. None for a range that is empty, reversed or not finite.
