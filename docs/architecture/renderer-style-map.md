# Network renderer style map

The first imported renderer (`472d0b5`, `mainwindow.cpp`) is the railway-symbol
reference. Preview, playback and replay share current scene geometry, inspection
and layer controls. Canonical authored track/node geometry supersedes historical
case-specific drawing branches.

| Primitive | Historical function (`472d0b5`) | Presentation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas | `myQGraphicsView` constructor: background `rgb(0, 0, 0)`, no grid. | Background `#101a22` with a faint `#182832` grid in preview, live simulation and replay. Grid lines are cosmetic one-device-pixel lines on multiples of the spacing in scene coordinates, so they move with the scene while panning. Spacing starts at 80 scene units and is doubled or halved until it is 24 to 96 pixels on screen. |
| Speed-class track | `arcDrawing` with `classifyTrackSpeed`: cosmetic pen of width `max(line_width, visual.width)`. | At least 200 km/h: RGB (30,130,210), cosmetic width 4; at least 120: RGB (160,160,160), width 3; otherwise RGB (120,120,120), width 2. |
| Connection | `paintConnection`: white, cosmetic, width `line_width`. The drawing code adds every connection before the track lines. | White cosmetic width 2, drawn below the track lines. |
| Segment/switch node | `paintNode`: light-gray ellipse of size `node_size`, cosmetic hairline pen; size 0 at the arc starts of a track with four nodes. In `Input/Input_EGTRAIN_Banedanmark` a track with four nodes draws only its middle arc and no last node. | Shared preview/runtime scene-sized light-gray circular dot, without a device-pixel minimum. The current runnable-chain rule uses an ordinary first point and zero painted size for ordinary starts on four-node tracks; the final endpoint remains visible. A track whose view sets `double_switch` paints no ordinary dot and draws neither its first nor its last arc. Transparent device-space hit geometry retains semantic picking without painting a minimum dot. |
| Station/platform node | `paintStationNode` with `classifyStation`; `paintStationPlatform` for the white platform bars in passenger-GUI mode. | Shared preview/runtime square at later station endpoints. A square with three or more connections is the interchange blue (80,120,210); the square at the end of a line with a platform is the platform dark gray (70,70,70); every other square, including an interior station square with a platform, is the ordinary gray (120,120,120). White platform bars follow passenger-GUI mode; preview has no passenger counters. A bar lies along its track at its node, centred 1.15 square sides from the node on the upper side of the track (on the left of a vertical track); its counter stays level above it and its passenger icons are spread along it. Station and name layers do not hide structural squares. In the near look the dots of the station mark cover the squares at the place of the station; a click on a square still selects the square. |
| Station name and pictogram | `paintStationName`: white text, font `size / 5`, a space before every capital after the first letter. `paintStationIcon`: raster `station_pixmap` scaled to `station_size`. | White name, font `station_size / 5`, and a mark, both at scene scale where the authored station anchor plus its derived decoration offset puts them. On a schematic line, one that the station views of its region place at a single latitude, the offset counts from level 0, as in the reference, and the offset of a station with a named nudge counts in the reference spacing of the levels, 150 historical units, so that its name stays between the same rows of track. On the canvas both are placed by the station squares at the place of the station: those at most 32 mark frames at scene size from its anchor, or the anchor alone without one, so the platform nodes of a station on another line get no dot and no area. The mark has two looks, both light (232,238,242) with a dark (16,26,34) halo of 2 logical pixels and painted as vectors at the device resolution. The near look applies once the tracks at the stations are at least 12 logical pixels apart on screen, measured as the median, over the stations with two or more squares, of the smallest distance between two of their squares. The far mark is a roundel in a square frame whose bottom centre stands on the top edge of the box of the squares, centred over them: a ring of outer radius 0.36 and width 0.12 of the frame for a halt (no platform), the ring with a blue (80,120,210) bar of 1.02 by 0.21 of the frame across it for a station (at least one platform), and a disc with the bar for a major station (three or more connections at its node). The bar has an edge in the halo colour. Under the tracks the far look draws the area of the station: the convex hull of the centres of its squares, widened by 6 logical pixels on every side, in the bar blue at an alpha of 56 of 255. The area takes no clicks, shows and hides with the mark and is hidden in the near look. The near mark covers the squares: a dot of radius 0.22 frame on each square of a station, stacked in node order, one dot of 0.15 frame on the first square of a halt, and the hull of the squares widened by 0.23 frame for a major station, each with an edge of 0.07 frame in the halo colour except the dot of a halt. The dots are sized by the frame and drawn on the squares without an item transform. A run without the shared preview does not count connections, so it draws no major station. The mark is parented to its authored structural node where available for artwork hits; the far mark is picked on its frame and the near mark on its dots. The name goes a quarter frame under the squares, centred on the mark, in both looks. Where its text there crosses a track line or the mark of another station, it goes to the first free spot of: above the mark, right of the squares and left of them; with none free it stays under the squares. The frame is at least 24 px on screen and the name font at least 12 px; above that the scene size applies. Names that overlap on screen are hidden in order of selected, followed, platform count, then the existing tie breaks, and a name gives way to the mark of another station, taken as the area that the mark covers in either look, so that the same names show in both looks; the mark and its hit target stay. Preview artwork keeps its own canonical station identity; position-only artwork has no invented node. The map key has one row for each class on the canvas ("Major station", "Station", "Halt"), each with the near mark on two squares, one for a halt, beside the roundel; the map key and menus use the marks in a dark variant. |
| Signal | `paintSignal`: white post and base, head of size `node_size` with a hairline pen (width 0), one head per direction. Transparent device-space hit geometry retains signal inspection at Fit: a click on it selects the signal even where a track or connection lies under it, while station artwork keeps its priority. Selection does not overwrite aspects. A head has one of five states, with one text for the tooltip, the inspector and the end-to-end checks: Stop (red, codes 0 and 751), Caution (yellow, code 75), Proceed (green, codes 180 and 270), Unavailable (an empty gray ring, RGB (150,150,150)) and Failed (a red lamp with a white cross). A head is unavailable when its section has no signalling level, when no route runs through its section in its direction, and for a code that no aspect uses. A head is failed, on every head of the failed section that has a state, for the steps in which a signal failure incident is active. Levels 3 and 4 keep red and green. Heads in the preview before a run stay green. A lit head (every state except Unavailable, whose gray ring has its own stroke) has a black outline of one device pixel at every zoom. ## Intentional differences from the historical renderer

These are the only intentional differences. A difference from the reference that is not listed here is not intended. The numbers are the pull requests that made the change, or the issue where none did; [Historical track presentation](historical-track-comparison.md) gives the detail and the captures.

- Station pictogram and its size on screen (#579, #822, #1063): a mark per station class with a dark halo replaces the raster pictogram, and the pictogram and the name have a minimum size on screen. Zoomed out the mark is a roundel over a faint area that covers the station squares, zoomed in a dot on each square. The name sits under the station, or off the tracks above the mark or beside the squares, where the reference centred it on the anchor.
- Canvas (#509): a soft background and a faint grid replace solid black.
- Selection (#606): a ring replaces the blue recolouring of the selected item.
- Train colours (#549, #555, #572): one default colour replaces the keyword colours, and a service can set its own colour on the canvas and in the result windows.
- Signal head states (#586): an empty ring for a head without signalling data, a red lamp with a white cross for a failed head, and a dark bar or dot on Stop and Caution. A lit head keeps the hairline outline of the reference.
- Presentation scale (#422): symbol sizes, text and offsets are converted from the historical construction units.
- Mainline track grey (#822): track of 120 km/h and above is (160,160,160) instead of (80,80,80), which is below the 3:1 contrast target.
- Train size on screen (#822): a train body is at least 8 logical pixels on screen, so a running train stays visible at Fit. Signal heads keep their 6 pixel rule.
- Station names (issue #387): a space goes before a capital only after a lowercase letter or at the end of an acronym.
- Platform bars (issue #1060): a bar lies along its track, where the reference drew every bar level above its node.
- Train speed: the speed beside a train is white with a dark outline, keeps its size on screen and is shown from a zoom of 3x or for a selected or followed train. The reference drew a black label at scene scale beside every running train.
- Track states (issue #387): blocked and prepared sections are shown in the inspector and not painted. An occupied section is plain red (255,0,0) at the width of its speed class, where the reference tinted it with a red colour effect.
- Running state (issue #387): the white repaint of every track at each step is not reproduced.

## Contrast and presentation notes

(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)). The grid is deliberately close to the canvas (1.16:1).

Operational prepared and blocked states remain in scene data and inspectors,
but do not change ordinary historical track paint; an occupied line is red in a
run and a replay. Selection draws a
ring around the item and never recolours it, so a selected track keeps its
speed-class color. The exception is the preview, where the track of the row selected in the Infrastructure table is
drawn in the cue colour, width 4. A refresh of the table keeps a selected row and selects none otherwise, so Run, Stop, Save and New case
draw no track in it unless the user selected a row. The cue colour is reserved: no built-in track, signal or default train colour uses
it; a service colour chosen by the user is not checked. The map key shows only visible track styles. Layer/Follow/replay controls are
unchanged.

Preview uses canonical read-only fields and marks runtime-only information
unavailable before Run. Signal head identity is the rendered canonical track,
section and direction, plus any authored signal IDs, not a numeric runtime
placeholder. Runtime numeric inspection remains separate. Expanded hit shapes
do not determine preview topology bounds or Fit. These implementation and
semantic checks do not establish matched-scale historical visual acceptance.

Scene-space presentation distances use the fixed measured historical
construction convention `Q_h = 800000 * 100 / 360`, converted by TrackPreview's
actual normalization `S_c / Q_h`. This includes artwork/text geometry and
annotation nudges, node/signal dimensions, platform/passenger adornments, train
lateral thickness and callouts. It does not rescale physical interpolation or
cosmetic strokes. Fonts retain their original metrics through fractional item
scaling. Only wholly successful visible authored station-view projections enable
conversion; raw, mixed, invalid, empty, degenerate or nonfinite scenes keep factor
1. See [Historical track presentation](historical-track-comparison.md) for the
reference convention and remaining visual acceptance limits.
